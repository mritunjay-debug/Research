Underground Pillar Stability Lab

Explore a configurable room-and-pillar layout, compare material-aware empirical screening estimates, and export reproducible results. Use the validation bench to assess model predictions against independent observations.

Engineering scope: this is an interactive geometry viewer and simplified analytical screening calculation—not a finite-element/finite-difference solver or certified pillar design method. It represents a regular rectangular pillar grid with tributary-area loading; the room openings are independently configurable along X and Y. UCS/stress ratio is not a factor of safety or a validated pillar strength estimate. Validate any design using suitable empirical methods, numerical modelling, site data, and competent engineering review.

σv = γ × depth
σp = σv × ((W + Rx)(L + Ry) / (W × L))
Lunder–Pakalnis: Sp = 0.44 × UCS × (0.68 + 0.52κ)
Krauland–Soder: Sp = 0.354 × UCS × (0.778 + 0.222 × W/H)
Strength/stress ratio = selected empirical Sp ÷ estimated σp
W = pillar width X; L = pillar length Y; H = pillar height; Rx and Ry = room openings along X and Y; γ = unit weight. The Lunder–Pakalnis model uses a confinement term derived from W/H and is a hard-rock empirical formulation. For rectangular pillars, its strength estimate does not explicitly correct for plan aspect ratio. Krauland–Soder is shown as a limestone/skarn formulation. Neither is universally applicable to every material or mine geometry. Literature: Lunder & Pakalnis (1997); hard-rock pillar model comparison.

Model applicability and validation status

Included

  • Selectable rock material and editable UCS/unit weight presets.
  • Tributary-area stress estimate and two published empirical hard-rock strength formulations.
  • Strength/stress screening ratio, parametric chart, scenario comparison and exports.
  • Separate browser-local validation bench for observed-versus-predicted case histories and standard error metrics.

Not included or validated

  • Independent case-history validation is not supplied automatically; use the Pillar Model Validation Bench with measured data.
  • Finite-element or finite-difference stress redistribution.
  • Site-calibrated rock-mass strength or a certified design factor of safety.
  • Joint-controlled failure, anisotropy, plastic yielding, dynamic loading or excavation sequence.
  • Coal-specific pillar formula, irregular geometry, inclined seams, topography or open-pit interaction.
  • Independent calibration against field observations or numerical models.
Material presets are illustrative starting values, not recommended design properties or universal material constants. Replace them with laboratory/site data. The empirical models were calibrated for particular case-history populations; the material selector does not make a formula valid for a material outside its calibration domain. Do not use this prototype alone to approve mine layouts or set safe operating limits.
